# The bitmap art pipeline

Ajayeb's card, wonder, token, card-back, texture, hero, AI-avatar,
victory-scene and app-icon art is "illuminated garden": original,
Persian-miniature-influenced illustration, generated one image at a time by
Codex `image_gen` and reviewed before anything ships. This page is how the
pipeline runs end to end. The icon set (resources, science symbols,
category glyphs and ornaments) is a separate, hand-authored SVG pipeline;
see `art/vector/`.

Everything here follows [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)'s "Assets" rules:
Codex runs strictly one image at a time, under the machine-wide `codex`
lane (`python3 tools/heavy.py run --kind codex`); approved originals are
committed under `art/originals/` as lossless WebP, pixel-identical to the
generated PNG (see step 7); rejected attempts never
enter the repository; shipped bitmaps are AVIF only, under
`web/static/art/`, derived by `tools/art/process.mjs` and never hand-edited;
and the app references only approved art, through the generated
`web/src/lib/art/available.gen.ts`, drawing a procedural placeholder for
everything else.

**One exception to "AVIF only": the app icon PNGs.** iOS reads its home
screen icon from `<link rel="apple-touch-icon">` and the web app manifest
lists its icons, and both need PNG. So `web/static/icons/` holds
`apple-touch-icon.png` (180 px), `icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png`, made
from the approved `icon/app` original by
`node tools/art/process.mjs --app-icons` (see step 7) and linked from
`web/src/app.html` and `web/static/manifest.webmanifest`. Everything else
the app shows stays AVIF. The same goes for the two textures, which the
app names in CSS (`--table-texture` in `web/src/lib/styles/tokens.css`)
instead of through the index; a unit test checks they are approved art.

## Files

```
art/
  style.md              style prefix, palette, motifs, do/don't, kind templates
  subjects.json         human-written: one subject + accent per asset id
  manifest.json         generated: catalog + subjects.json, with status per asset
  refs/                 the owner's style anchors, one JPEG per kind, and a README
  originals/<kind>/<id>.webp  approved originals (committed, lossless WebP,
                              pixel-identical to the generated PNG)
  LICENSE                CC BY 4.0 notice

tools/art/
  lib.mjs          shared paths, the catalog reader, the kind/size table,
                   the raw-pixel helpers (crop, paper-colour, PAPER_HEX)
  manifest.mjs      rebuilds art/manifest.json
  generate.mjs      one Codex image_gen call per asset
  process.mjs       original -> shipped AVIF sizes, with budgets
  sheet.mjs         a contact sheet of chosen attempts (several ids, each
                     at one or more attempts)
  gate.mjs          objective paper and margin check: fails an attempt whose
                     margin doesn't read as its own paper colour, or whose
                     paper is too far from PAPER_HEX
  apply-review.mjs  applies a reviewer's decisions.jsonl
  index.mjs         art/manifest.json (approved only) -> available.gen.ts

web/src/lib/art/available.gen.ts   generated: approved ids -> shipped paths and sizes

${AJAYEB_RUN_DIR:-~/tmp/agents/ajayeb}/art-work/   outside the repo
  raw/<kind>/<slug>/<attempt>.png   every attempt, approved or not
  sheets/                           contact sheets
```

Where the UX design draft (`~/tmp/agents/ajayeb/design/ux.md` §4, §8) says
WebP, `scripts/art`, `apps/web` or `available.ts`, this repository's actual
decisions win instead: **AVIF only** (`avifenc`; the design's WebP sizes
and budgets carry over unchanged), `tools/art/*.mjs` as dependency-free
Node 24 ESM scripts (no bundler, no npm dependency), shipped files under
`web/static/art/<kind>/`, and the generated index at
`web/src/lib/art/available.gen.ts`.

## Asset ids and the asset table

Every asset has an id `<kind>/<slug>`: `card/lumber-yard`,
`wonder/pyramids`, `token/agriculture`, `back/age1`, `texture/paper`,
`hero/hero`, `avatar/apprentice`, `victory/military`, `icon/app`. Card,
wonder and token slugs come from the engine's presentation catalog
(`web/src/lib/wire/catalog.gen.ts`, generated by `make gen` from
`server/internal/engine/catalog`); the rest are a fixed list in
`tools/art/lib.mjs` (`STATIC_ASSETS`), since they have no catalog entry.

109 assets in total, matching the UX design's asset table:

- 73 cards (23 Age I, 23 Age II, 20 Age III, 7 guilds): generated
  1536x1024, cropped 4:3, shipped at 480x360 (<=35 KB) and a 240x180 thumb
  (<=10 KB)
- 12 wonders: 1536x1024, 3:2, shipped 960x640 (<=70 KB) and a 480x320
  thumb (<=20 KB)
- 10 progress tokens: 1024x1024, circle-masked onto the paper colour,
  shipped 256px (<=14 KB) and a 96px thumb (<=5 KB)
- 4 card backs (age1, age2, age3, guild): 1024x1536, 1:1.545, shipped
  320x495 (<=30 KB)
- 2 textures (paper, night): 1024x1024, tileable, shipped 512px (<=60 KB)
- 1 hero: 1536x1024, 16:9, shipped 1600x900 (<=120 KB) and an 800x450
  `hero@800` for phones (<=60 KB)
- 3 AI avatars (apprentice, scholar, master): 1024x1024, shipped 192px
  (<=12 KB)
- 3 victory scenes (military, science, civilian): 1536x1024, 16:9,
  shipped 960x540 (<=60 KB)
- 1 app icon: 1024x1024, shipped 512/192/180/32px AVIF (<=60/20/20/4 KB)

The exact numbers live in one place, `KIND_SPEC` in `tools/art/lib.mjs`, so
this list and the code cannot drift silently; check the code first if they
ever disagree.

## Running it, step by step

1. **`node tools/art/manifest.mjs`** rebuilds `art/manifest.json` from the
   catalog and `art/subjects.json`. Run it after a catalog change or a
   `subjects.json` edit. It never overwrites `subjects.json`, and it keeps
   every asset's existing status, attempt count, prompt amendment and
   shipped byte counts; it only adds missing ids and drops stale ones (with
   a warning). `--check` fails without writing, for a CI-style gate.

2. **`python3 tools/heavy.py run --kind codex -- node tools/art/generate.mjs --ids <id>`**
   generates one attempt for one asset:
   - builds the prompt from `art/style.md`'s verbatim style prefix, the
     kind's template (filled in with the manifest's `subject` and
     `accent`), and the asset's `promptAmend` if a review round rejected a
     previous attempt;
   - snapshots `${CODEX_HOME:-~/.codex}/generated_images/**`;
   - runs `codex exec --ephemeral --skip-git-repo-check -s read-only -C
     <scratch> --enable image_generation -o <scratch>/last.txt` with an
     instruction to call `image_gen` exactly once with the full prompt and
     reply `DONE`, under a 6 minute timeout;
   - finds the newest PNG that appeared since the snapshot (the script
     does this itself, never trusting the agent to report the path),
     **verifies it came back the requested shape** (the same aspect ratio,
     within 1%, and at least the requested size; a different shape is
     rejected outright: in the pilot a reference-image run returned a
     1254x1254 square for a 1536x1024 card, while every plain square
     request returns 1254x1254, which is accepted), and copies it to
     `art-work/raw/<kind>/<slug>/<attempt>.png`, numbered one past the
     larger of the manifest's counter and the highest attempt already on
     disk, so nothing generated earlier is ever overwritten;
   - records the attempt in the manifest (`status: "generated"`).

   `--kind KIND --limit N` (default 1) picks the next `N` assets of one
   kind whose status is `todo` or `rejected` (a rejected asset is retried
   with its stored `promptAmend`); `--status S` narrows that to one status.
   `--dry-run` prints the prompt and the exact `codex exec` command without
   running anything. `CODEX_BIN` defaults to `~/.local/bin/codex` (never a
   shell function of the same name).

   **A reference image is off by default.** `--ref` passes
   `art/refs/<kind>-anchor.{jpg,png}` to Codex as `--image=<path>`, when
   one exists, for a cross-subject style anchor. Without `--ref` (the
   default), no image is sent at all: the M9.B3 pilot found a *same-subject*
   reference makes Codex return a near-copy of the anchor rather than a new
   illustration, and the style prefix alone already keeps the look
   consistent across subjects.

   The `heavy.py run --kind codex` wrapper is what enforces "strictly one
   at a time" machine-wide, across scripts and sessions, per AGENTS.md.
   When `generate.mjs` is started without it, it re-executes itself under
   that wrapper, so the lane is never skipped. The instruction names the
   kind's generation size (landscape 1536x1024, square 1024x1024 or
   portrait 1024x1536).

3. **`node tools/art/gate.mjs --ids <id>,<id>,... --attempt all`** (or
   `--kind KIND`, or `--all` to sweep every attempt file on disk) runs the
   objective paper and margin check before a human ever looks at a
   sheet. It crops each attempt to its kind's main shipped size, finds
   the crop's own paper colour (the corner median), and runs two checks;
   an attempt passes only when both do:
   - **The band check** (relative) fails any attempt where less than half
     of the outer 4% band reads as that paper colour: a painting with no
     margin anywhere, "full-bleed", reads badly once the card or wonder
     frame feathers into it.
   - **The paper check** (absolute, added in `M9.B4`) fails any attempt
     whose detected paper is more than a CIEDE2000 difference of 18 from
     `PAPER_HEX` (`#f4ecdc`). The band check alone compares the edge with
     the image's own corners, so an attempt with gold-leaf corners passed
     it as if gold were its paper (`wonder/colossus` attempt 1 reads
     paper `rgb(212,165,84)`). CIEDE2000, a perceptual colour difference,
     is used instead of RGB distance so one threshold means about the same
     visible step anywhere in the ivory-to-gold range.

   The threshold was calibrated on all 30 pilot attempts plus the one
   surviving thumbnail of the lost `card/theater` attempt 1. Every paper a
   reviewer accepts sits at 14.2 or less (`wonder/colossus` attempt 2, a
   warm sand about 83 RGB from `PAPER_HEX`, is the 14.2; every card and
   token is at 10.8 or less). The gold and brown grounds sit at 21.8 or
   more (the theater thumbnail 21.8, `wonder/colossus` attempt 1 23.6,
   `wonder/pyramids` attempt 3 27.9). 18 is the middle of that gap.
   `wonder/pyramids` attempt 1 and `wonder/hanging-gardens` attempt 3 have
   an ivory-enough paper (13.5 and 13.6) and fail the band check instead.
   `--paper-max N` moves the threshold (0 turns the paper check off).

   This is a margin and ground check only, not a review; a pass here is
   necessary, not sufficient, for approval. `--json` prints one line of
   JSON per attempt instead of a table (with `paperDE` and the list of
   checks that `failed`); `--strict` exits 1 if anything failed, for
   scripting.

4. **`node tools/art/sheet.mjs --ids <id>,<id>,... [--name NAME]`** (or
   `--kind KIND` for every generated asset of that kind) builds a
   numbered, labelled contact sheet with ImageMagick `montage`, written to
   `art-work/sheets/<name>.jpg`. An id may carry `#<attempt>` (a number,
   `latest` or `all`) to put a specific attempt, or every attempt, of that
   id on the sheet, so several attempts of several ids can sit on one
   sheet, e.g. `--ids card/theater#1,card/theater#2,card/theater#3` to
   compare three rejected variants, or `--ids card/theater --attempt all`
   for the same thing without naming each attempt. Look at it (and,
   ideally, the individual PNGs) before writing a review decision.

5. **Review**: write a `decisions.jsonl`, one JSON object per line:
   `{"id": "card/lumber-yard", "attempt": 1, "verdict": "approve"}`,
   `{"id": "card/lumber-yard", "attempt": 1, "verdict": "reject",
   "reasons": ["subject too small at thumbnail size"], "promptAmend":
   "larger, closer central subject"}` or `{"id": "token/law", "attempt": 2,
   "verdict": "needs-human", "reasons": ["the subject needs rewriting"]}`.
   This file is not committed; it is a reviewer's scratch input to the
   next step.

6. **`node tools/art/apply-review.mjs path/to/decisions.jsonl`** applies
   it to `art/manifest.json`:
   - `approve` sets `status: "approved"` and records the decision's attempt
     as `approvedAttempt`. It may be an **earlier attempt than the
     latest**, when the reviewer prefers it to every retry since; its raw
     file must exist. `process.mjs` and `index.mjs` then use that attempt
     and print which one they used.
   - `reject` sets `status: "rejected"` (with the `promptAmend` stored for
     the next `generate.mjs` run) unless the asset has already had
     `--max-attempts` attempts (default 3), in which case it becomes
     `needs-human`.
   - `needs-human` sets that status directly, with the reasons, whatever
     the attempt count: for an asset no prompt amendment will fix, such as
     a subject that needs rewriting or a choice only the owner can make.

   A rejection whose `attempt` does not match the manifest's current
   attempt is skipped as stale (a newer attempt exists that the reviewer
   never saw). An approval or a `needs-human` may name an earlier attempt,
   but never one past the current. `--manifest PATH` applies the decisions
   to a scratch copy of the manifest instead, and `--dry-run` writes
   nothing. A new `generate.mjs` attempt clears `approvedAttempt`, and
   `manifest.mjs` carries `approvedAttempt` and `reasons` through a
   rebuild.

7. **`node tools/art/process.mjs --id <id>`** turns an **approved**
   attempt into the shipped AVIF sizes. With no `--src` it processes the
   approved attempt's raw file,
   `art-work/raw/<kind>/<slug>/<approvedAttempt>.png`, and says so; a
   `--src` that is a different raw attempt of the same id is refused. For each
   shipped size it crops to the exact pixel size (the same "fill" crop
   `gate.mjs` checks: `-resize WxH^ -gravity center -extent WxH`), reads
   the crop back as raw RGB bytes, then does two pixel-level fixes before
   handing it to `avifenc`:
   - **paper-tone mapping**: finds the crop's own paper colour (the
     corner-median) and maps it onto `PAPER_HEX` (`#f4ecdc`, the app's
     `--card-paper` token) with a smooth per-pixel falloff, so only
     paper-like pixels move and the painting's other colours are
     untouched. A generated gouache ground runs warmer than the CSS
     token; without this it shows as a visible seam against the
     surrounding card or token frame.
   - **token rim detection** (progress tokens only): instead of always
     masking to a circle that touches the square's edges, it walks inward
     from each of the 4 edges to find the medallion's own painted rim and
     masks to that (with a 1.5px antialiased edge), so a medallion painted
     with a margin inside the square doesn't leave a visible ivory "halo"
     ring once masked.

   `avifenc` then encodes the result, stepping quality down (55 → 45 →
   35) until the asset table's byte budget holds or the lowest quality is
   reached (reported either way), and records the shipped byte counts in
   the manifest.

   Before any of that, it writes the committed original,
   `art/originals/<kind>/<slug>.webp`, as **lossless WebP** (`cwebp
   -lossless -exact -z 9`, falling back to ImageMagick's
   `webp:lossless=true`), and proves it is pixel-identical to the source:
   `magick compare -metric AE` (all channels, alpha included) must count 0
   differing pixels, and the two files' raw 16-bit RGBA values must match
   byte for byte, which also catches a changed colour under a transparent
   pixel or a source deeper than 8 bits. Any difference is a loud failure
   before anything ships. The owner chose lossless WebP over PNG
   (`M9.B4`) to keep the public repository smaller: on the pilot it is
   about 70% of the PNG's size (`card/scriptorium` attempt 2: 2.25 MB
   against 3.19 MB, 0 differing pixels), and `-z 9` costs about 20 seconds
   per image. `--src` may itself be a `.webp` original; the AVIFs made from
   it are byte-identical to those made from the PNG.

   **The asset's manifest status must already be
   `"approved"`** (set by `apply-review.mjs`); use `--out DIR` to
   process into a scratch directory instead, which skips that check,
   writes the original to `DIR/originals/<kind>/<slug>.webp` and the AVIFs
   under `DIR`, and leaves the manifest and `art/originals/`/
   `web/static/art/` untouched.
   This is how a pipeline change or a pilot run is proved without shipping
   or approving anything.

   **`--app-icons`** is a separate mode for the app icon PNGs: it reads
   the committed original `art/originals/icon/app.webp` (the manifest must
   have `icon/app` approved), crops and paper-maps it exactly as the AVIF
   sizes are, and writes `web/static/icons/apple-touch-icon.png` (180 px),
   `icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png`, or the same names under `--out DIR`.
   The gouache grain makes a truecolour PNG large (408 KB at 512 px), so
   each is quantised to a 256-colour palette with Floyd-Steinberg
   dithering (91 KB at 512 px, 11 KB at 180 px, no visible change at icon
   sizes). It touches neither the manifest nor `web/static/art/`.

   **Known issue: the night texture ships pale.** The paper-tone mapping
   moves every image's own ground (its corner colour) onto `PAPER_HEX`,
   and the ground of `texture/night` is the deep lapis paper itself, so
   the shipped `ui/night.avif` came out as ivory with blue fibres (the
   original in `art/originals/` is lapis). The app works around it by
   multiplying the tile over a slightly lifted lapis (`tokens.css`), which
   keeps only its grain. Skipping the mapping for that asset and
   reprocessing it would ship the lapis tile; the dark theme's
   `--table-texture-blend` then becomes `normal` over `--table`.

8. **`node tools/art/index.mjs`** regenerates
   `web/src/lib/art/available.gen.ts` from every `"approved"` asset in the
   manifest: an id, its kind, and its shipped variants' paths, pixel sizes
   and byte counts. An id the app looks up here and doesn't find has no
   approved art yet, and the app's procedural placeholder draws instead
   (`AGENTS.md` "Assets"): nothing ever 404s.

## Review criteria

An attempt is approved only when all of these hold, checked at full
resolution and at the shipped thumbnail size (cards at 120 px, tokens at
40 px, matching the render tiers in `design/ux.md` §4):

- **Passes `gate.mjs`** (step 3 above): the attempt has a real margin of
  its own paper colour after the shipping crop, not a painting that runs
  to every edge, and that paper is ivory, not gold leaf or brown. This is checked before the sheet is
  even built, so a full-bleed attempt is caught before a human looks at it.
- No text, letters, numerals, pseudo-writing or watermark anywhere in the
  image, including as a pedestal frieze, a ruled page or a carved wall.
- The subject reads clearly at the shipped thumbnail size, not just at full
  resolution; one or two large figures, not a crowd.
- The palette and accent match the category (see `art/style.md`'s palette
  section).
- The style matches the anchors in `art/refs/` (line, palette, paper
  margin and density; see `art/refs/README.md` for what each one anchors
  and what it does not).
- The paper background feathers cleanly; nothing looks like a hard
  rectangle waiting to be masked.
- Background architecture reads as palaces, domed garden pavilions and
  kiosks, never minarets or mosques, unless the asset's own `subject`
  names one.
- Culturally respectful, per `art/style.md`'s avoid list: no religious
  figures, no political or national emblems, no heraldic animals on
  buildings or banners, no anachronisms.
- No filler animals (deer, peacocks, a row of cypress trees) unless the
  asset's own `subject` names one.
- The shipped byte budget is met (or the quality floor is hit and the
  owner is told).

## The pilot

Before a batch run, a small pilot proves prompts and anchors work: a few
subjects per kind, a contact sheet, a human pick of the style anchors (into
`art/refs/`), then the batch. `M9.B0` (this slice) proves the mechanics
end to end with one real card generation, one contact sheet and one
scratch `process.mjs` run, but approves nothing.

`M9.B1`/`M9.B2` ran the actual 27-image style pilot (3 subjects x 3
variants for card, wonder and token: `lumber-yard`, `scriptorium`,
`theater`; `pyramids`, `colossus`, `hanging-gardens`; `agriculture`,
`law`, `theology`). `M9.B3` applied an art critic's pass over those 27
images to `art/style.md` and `art/subjects.json` (the gold rule, the card
and wonder composition rules, the token template rewrite, tighter text and
emblem negatives, a jewel accent per wonder, a distinct accent per token,
and rewritten Colossus and Theater subjects; the Pyramids subject was
left for the owner, and `M9.B4` set it to the smooth-sided pyramids of
Giza, never stepped) and to `tools/art/process.mjs`
(the paper-tone mapping and the token rim mask) and `generate.mjs`
(reference images off by default, output shape verified), and added
`gate.mjs`. Proved on the existing pilot images plus 2 new generations
(`card/theater`, `wonder/colossus`); a third (`token/law`) was rejected 3
times by a first, exact-size version of the check, because `image_gen`
returns every square request as 1254x1254 (all 9 pilot tokens are that
size). The review relaxed the check to the aspect ratio plus a minimum
size, which still rejects the pilot's square-for-landscape failure.

## Throughput

About 109 assets, 1-2 minutes each for `generate.mjs` plus whatever
rejections cost, so a full batch is a few hours sequentially, which is fine
overnight, under `nightshift` mode (see `AGENTS.md` "Owner phrases").
