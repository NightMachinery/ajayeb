## Proposed decision entries

These are draft names, not reserved D-numbers. The sections below supply their mechanics and acceptance checks.

- **Register room implementations.** Share lifecycle and transport mechanisms, with separate typed actors. This limits Duel regressions; reject a universal Engine/Factory, capability matrix and dual transaction modes inside one actor.
- **Resolve complete decision batches.** The engine owns simultaneous semantics and ability order. Durable, changeable pending picks support reconnect without making arrival order canonical; reject partial engine mutation and append-per-arrival rules history.
- **Keep compatibility in per-game codecs and frame families.** Preserve schema-1 Duel and its game DTOs. Reject automatic migration, normalized Duel records, metadata discovery messages and a second Duel wire contract.
- **Project by whitelist for every viewer.** Hands, offers, remembered knowledge and pending picks belong only to their authorized seat. Reject full-state redaction by deletion and shared private hint objects.
- **Use one mixed-seat lobby.** Start freezes seat assignment and policy inputs. The admin's seat switches change control, never topology or victory; reject separate bot/online modes and readiness revisions.
- **Give each bot fixed work and permitted information.** Persist effective budgets and policy versions. Reject splitting one room allowance among bots, full-state-derived seeds and a new global CPU/memory scheduler.
- **Preserve Duel client and cache behavior.** Select a lazy game entry from the authoritative snapshot. Reject genericizing Duel Session and changing precache before install-transfer evidence exists.
- **Measure Haftgane capacity and privacy.** Extend tests to concurrent seats and all viewer roles while preserving Duel baselines. Reject invented room-memory reservations, payload limits and room caps.
- **Share one online lobby with Duel (awaiting the owner).** Two filled seats at Start create a Duel game, three to seven a Haftgane game. Reject separate Home entries for online play and the removal of Duel's one-tap starts.

## Owner decisions

The owner answered these points on 2026-10-21. Each paragraph stays the single policy point for its topic; implementation sections refer to these names.

**Commitment, decided.** A pick stays changeable until the last required seat confirms. While others are still choosing, a seat may replace its pick or withdraw its confirmation; the confirmation that completes the required set resolves the point at once, and a change that arrives after it is refused with `decision-closed`, which the client explains as "everyone had confirmed; your confirmed pick was played". Reload and reconnect restore the seat's current pick and its confirmed state. Others see only who has confirmed, never what was picked or how often it changed. The existing `onlineOnly`, `always`, `never` confirmation setting still governs the confirm tap; for this game `onlineOnly` means the game started with another human seat.

**Memory, decided.** A private Seen hands field in the owning seat's view, containing only hands that seat actually held, marked by historical point, with summary statistics computed from the same knowledge (engine-architecture.md, section 10). Bots remember the same observations and may derive the same statistics, so human and bot knowledge stay equal. These are view fields, not a separate private history channel; remembering a card does not reveal its current location.

**Admin powers, decided; details provisional.** The admin may switch any seat between human and bot control at any time. A human replaced by a bot becomes a spectator, and the seat's pick, confirmed or not, is kept and confirmed for the bot. The admin may also seat any spectator in a bot's seat; that human takes over the seat's pick, still changeable, and the seat's remembered knowledge. Provisional details for the owner to confirm: a former player may return only to their own former seat, because they know that seat's hands; if all the admin's connections have been absent for two minutes, the admin role passes to the connected human who has held a seat longest; every switch shows in the game log for everyone. There is no automatic takeover of a disconnected seat and no connected-but-idle deadline: the admin decides. These powers apply to Haftgane games only.

**Seating, decided.** Random seating by default, with the admin able to choose lobby order before Start. The engine resolves the chosen assignment once during setup and persists it, with no reroll on reconnect or restore.

**Undo, decided, extension of D15.** Undo only in a game that started with exactly one human seat, unless the creator disabled it. Freeze `startingHumanCount` and `allowUndo` at Start. Undo turns off for the rest of the game as soon as a second human takes a seat (see Admin powers), and no later resignation or switch turns it back on. Eligible undo replays from setup to before the latest completed batch containing the human's decision and clears the pending block; an unresolved pick needs no undo, because it stays changeable. Undo after reveal exposes the bots' simultaneous picks, and deterministic bots repeat them; this is D15's accepted solo trade. It never changes a seat's controller or rerolls the deal.

**Bot quality, decided.** Equal fixed work per bot at a given difficulty at every table size. Six bots use three times the work of two bots, and their serialized work increases turn latency. For illustration only, six 150 ms searches take about 0.9 seconds before queueing and other overhead; no such timing has been measured. Calibrate a full table first.

## Decision points and acceptance

A **logical point** is the deterministic engine id and required seat set for a frozen position. A **wire point reference** identifies that opening of the point, including a fresh reference when undo reopens it. A **batch** contains exactly one key per required seat, sorted by seat. A **pending pick** is accepted but not resolved. The existing **transport revision** orders durable room changes, independently of canonical engine history.

The actor opens a point, freezes its engine value and derives each authorized seat's legal set and permitted observation from it. A simultaneous point may require all seats; a later ability point may require one. Only the engine decides that sequence, neighbour semantics, automatic transitions and resolution event order. Accepted pending picks never alter anyone else's offer or observation.

Submission is `{seq, point, key, confirm}`: it sets the seat's pick to `key` and its confirmed flag to `confirm`, so withdrawing a confirmation resends the current key with `confirm` false. The connection's current membership supplies the acting seat. There are no seat tokens, controller epochs, room lifetime counters or pending hashes. The actor checks authorization, its bounded per-user/client seq deduplication, current point reference, whether the seat is required, then membership in that seat's frozen legal set. An old or already resolved point yields `decision-closed`; an unoffered key yields `illegal`; a seq older than the one that last changed the slot from the same client yields `stale`, so a delayed or evicted request never reverts a newer pick. Rejects go only to the submitter and disclose no opponent information. Repeating an accepted seq is harmless. A bot's pick is confirmed when it is accepted.

Derive the wire point reference from the logical id and the existing durable revision at opening; persist that reference while the point remains open. Acceptance/presence updates do not replace it. Advancing or undoing opens a fresh reference, and restart preserves the saved reference. Only the logical id goes into canonical batches and bot entropy. This closes delayed-request replay after undo without adding a second generation counter. Validate no revision wrap or reuse. The evidence for this narrow deviation from the review is recorded below.

The engine's `Legal` is exactly the offered list, not a superset that the client reconstructs. Keys distinguish meaningful payment outcomes, such as coins paid to each neighbour, while equivalent producer assignments collapse to one key. Retain only nondominated outcomes: no offered pair is at least as costly to both neighbours and strictly costlier to one than another valid pair. The server applies the engine's own matching action, never a reconstructed client action.

Require a tested bound, not an arbitrary frame ceiling. If the verified catalog's largest construction buys at most U resource units and each unit costs at most two coins, left payment is 0..2U. For each left value retain only minimum right payment, then Pareto-prune, giving at most `2U+1` outcomes per construction. With at most seven cards, one building and one next-stage option per card plus sale give at most `14*(2U+1)+7` main-point keys. Separate side/ability offers are bounded by their catalog sets. The rules author must confirm these assumptions and publish tested constants; rules.md ruling 10 already accepts only an offered outcome. No private draft catalog values are needed for this proof.

## Persistence, replay and compatibility

Keep schema 1 on today's Duel struct and codec. Schema 2 is a Haftgane codec with explicit `game` and game rules version. Dispatch using a bounded header before game decoding or replay. There is no normalized common saved-room record and no automatic migration. The production schema-2 table starts empty; enable its codec before enabling creation.

The new record stores validated setup/options/seed, fixed initial assignment, original participants, current controllers/admin, lifecycle/rematch metadata, starting policy inputs, canonical complete batches, rules hash, current point reference, the private pending block (each required seat's current pick and confirmed flag), the undo-off flag and the public log of seat switches. Persist the bounded seq acknowledgements and bot policy/work ledger separately from rules history. Do not store a pending hash, opaque engine checkpoint, retained bot-result cache or redundant controller generation. Runtime disconnect clocks and bot task generation are not durable fields.

A Haftgane transaction clones the current value, validates the operation, atomically persists the candidate, then swaps and publishes it. A pick change or confirmation that leaves a required seat unconfirmed writes the pending slot, acknowledgement and next revision. The confirmation that completes the required set appends one complete sorted batch, resolves automatic transitions, clears pending and installs the next point/result in the same write. Never publish a reveal that has not been persisted. Storage failure discards the candidate and reports a temporary private error; it does not launch successor work. Start, undo, seat switches, admin handover, lobby edits and rematch linkage use the same discipline. Duel retains its current save/retry behavior (`server/internal/room/room.go:607`).

After a crash between rename and acknowledgement, replay restores acceptance and the retry cannot resolve twice. Restore validates setup, every complete batch's point/required seats/key membership, resulting rules hash, current point, each incomplete pending slot and controller/work-ledger consistency. A pending block in which every required seat has confirmed, a duplicate seat, impossible point or unknown key is invalid. Do not apply pending picks sequentially to the engine. No bot resumes until a human participant is attached; mere spectators cannot keep bot computation alive.

Canonical batch bytes and rules hashes depend on setup and logical batches, not seqs, revisions, timestamps, pick arrival order or task scheduling. Whole room-file bytes can differ because metadata differs. Undo cuts before the latest completed batch containing that human's decision and clears the pending block; an unresolved pick is changed, not undone. Replay from setup drops subsequent batches/pending picks and rebuilds deterministic work inputs; no stored checkpoint or bot-result retention is needed. Only the original sole human while still controlling their seat may invoke eligible undo. Seat switches are control changes and are never rolled back by rules undo.

Preserve D28's tuple `(schema/codec, game, game rules version)`. Known compatible legal replay plus valid pending is canonical; a hash-only mismatch refreshes atomically while preserving activity, identity, settings and unknown additive fields. Known incompatible rules archive as `rules-version`; known-game invalid replay/pending archives as `replay-failed`. Unknown schema, codec or game remains untouched and unsupported, never archived as corrupt Duel. Setup, randomness, catalog or key changes that affect replay require that game's rules-version bump. Wire and bot policy versions have separate purposes.

Keep D29's per-code serialization, live/archive layout, no-overwrite moves and retention bounds: live 7/14-day policies and diagnostics 30 days/1000 files/64 MiB. Unsupported files are not silently reaped. The codec supplies enough validated metadata for existing retention logic; malformed headers fail closed. Golden tests must show both hash-refresh preservation and unsupported-file byte preservation.

`CheckDataDir` uses the same codec table and existing aggregate counters. Keep the preflight report format, scalar `rules` field and `self_host.py` unchanged. That scalar continues to report Duel's version, not dispatch other games. The old binary already counts schema 2 as an error (`server/internal/store/store.go:170`, `server/internal/store/check.go:74`); the installer refuses errors even under force (`self_host.py:569`). Retain running/stopped scans and failed-second-scan recovery. Test rollback refusal while any unsupported live file exists; no new supportedSchemas map, per-game report or legacy-candidate classification is required.

Duel export remains `ajayeb-log/1`. New `ajayeb-log/2` contains game/rules/setup, complete canonical batches and strict final rules hash, without seqs, arrivals, work ledgers or transport references. Never provide a running-game seed or raw log download. Default end policy reveals all and permits download, matching Duel; a creation option disabling final reveal also disables download because sold/tucked keys expose hidden cards. The engine/projection contract must support that view policy rather than unconditionally reveal at end.

## Views, events and history

Build whitelist projections for each seated viewer and one spectator role. Use a fresh cache scoped to a single broadcast, as Duel's frame cache does (`server/internal/room/frames.go:25`); share only genuinely identical role/seat projections. Compose safe room metadata with the engine's typed view. No long-lived invalidation keys or full-state serialization are needed.

Public fields include seats/controllers, seat-based admin, presence, point kind/required seats, confirmed flags, lifecycle, public cities, coins and resolved action kinds/payments. Confirmed flags and the timing of their changes are intentionally public. A seat's unconfirmed changes are not: frames to other viewers carry nothing that moves with them, such as a revision that a private edit advances. The owner sees only their own hand, offered keys/costs, current pick with its confirmed flag and permitted historical knowledge. Apply Memory to Seen hands and its statistics. A private discard offer appears only during the owning seat's ability, with only legitimately retained pile knowledge afterward. A spectator or former controller receives none of these private fields.

Client-visible cards use catalog design ids, repeated for indistinguishable copies if necessary. If copy markers are ever required, they are immutable catalog markers, never shuffle/deal indices. Sort every hand and discard offer canonically, including observations, reconnect and history views. No ordering, instance id or key may encode a shuffled packet position. The engine draft's multiset design identity already supports this boundary.

Resolved events/history describe public outcomes, not raw submitted keys. A tucked or sold card's identity stays private until the configured end reveal. A late joiner/resync gets the same whitelist as live play, with a full safe history where needed. A former human controller immediately becomes a spectator, including on still-open tabs. Rematch linkage does not confer private access to the old room. Never attach seed, full fingerprint, observation hash, candidate diagnostics or opponent choices to a frame.

Ship no Haftgane hint families or advisor initially. If added later, D23 ownership stays with the admin, absence does not block play, and any hand-dependent calculation occurs inside that seat's projection using only its permitted information. Duel's existing hint/review paths stay unchanged. Do not reuse its shared hint object for private hands.

The Haftgane actor must not log unrevealed keys, hands, observations, seeds or low-entropy hashes. Log only safe operation kinds, public seat/revision context and bounded error codes; route private bot diagnostics to controlled test tooling rather than ordinary logs. Add log-capture acceptance tests with hidden sentinels. Duel's current key logging is not changed by this assignment.

## Lobby, admin powers and rematch

Creation specifies game, seat count and options, with no Haftgane mode. The creator occupies one lobby slot and is admin. Humans joining by link take unreserved empty slots; otherwise they spectate. The admin may add/remove bots in any empty slot, change supported setup options and remove a lobby human to spectator. Humans may release their own slot. Actor serialization, membership and lifecycle checks settle concurrent edits; there are no ready flags or roster revision.

Only the admin can Start, while occupying a human slot. Every seat must be filled; an explicit Start option fills empty seats with bots atomically. Apply Seating and freeze the initial assignment, starting human count, undo flag and bot configuration. An unclaimed human reservation is not a filled seat and cannot silently become a bot: the admin first opens that reservation. Start is the consent event, so a connected but inattentive human may be started; this is the accepted simplification versus readiness handshakes. Solo creation uses this same lobby and Start path.

A dropped socket detaches a connection, not its seat. A seat is disconnected only when all of its authorized connections are absent; it then shows as away, and the admin decides whether to switch it to a bot. The admin handover in Admin powers uses one monotonic runtime clock over the admin's connections; after restart, begin a fresh absence interval rather than infer old absence from file timestamps. Every switch enters the public game log with its kind (to a bot, to a human, resignation, admin handover), never private claims or addresses. Voluntary resignation immediately makes that seat a bot, with confirmation. No switch changes neighbours, ends the game or awards a surrender victory.

A switch to a bot keeps the seat's pick and confirms it; a seat without a pick gets a bot observation from the same frozen point and a persisted fixed work allocation. Revoke the former human's acting authority and claims for that seat; actor membership rejects their in-flight submissions. A switch to a human gives the seated spectator that seat's membership and private view from the next frame, cancels any bot task for it and leaves the pick changeable. No controller epoch is needed: membership is checked when each request arrives, and a stale seq cannot revert a newer pick. A former player may be seated again only in their own former seat (provisional), and can always spectate and join a rematch. Bots pause when no human participant is attached; an original participant watching after resignation may let the bot continuation finish. Unrelated spectators cannot trigger search.

There is no connected-stall takeover. The admin's mid-game role is the seat switches above and future hint settings, neither of which blocks play; if the admin is away, the provisional handover passes the role on. Any authorized original participant can request the single idempotent successor room after completion, so a missing old admin does not prevent a rematch. The requester becomes admin of that fresh lobby, not the old game.

The successor keeps the seat count, Start-time bot slots and setup options; replacement bots do not overwrite original-human reservations. Reserve each original human's slot for that participant until Start; reconnect claims rejoin those reservations, including humans replaced by bots in the old game. The new admin may explicitly open a reservation to another human or bot. Multiple rematch requests return one code under per-code serialization. A solo client's convenience flow may immediately issue ordinary Start once its successor lobby is filled; there is no second create/start mode or readiness protocol.

## Shared lobby, awaiting the owner

The owner asked whether two players should simply get Duel, and whether the lobby and its settings differ between the two games. The next paragraph is the single choice point for that question; an answer changes it, and the mechanics below follow it.

**Default, provisional.** Home keeps Duel's one-tap starts unchanged: play the bot, the tutorial, and pass and play. The online start ("play with friends") opens one shared lobby of 2 to 7 seats. The admin sets the seat count and may change it until Start; a seat removed while occupied returns its human to spectating. Haftgane's solo games against bots use the same lobby. The lobby shows the settings of the game its seat count selects: with two seats Duel's (who starts, random by default, and a bot seat's level), with three or more Haftgane's (Seating and its other options).

Start runs under the code's serialization: it validates the filled seats, writes the chosen game's room file under the same code (schema 1 for Duel, schema 2 for Haftgane) in place of the lobby file, and swaps the actor, so open connections attach to the new room and the next snapshot's frame family routes each client; a Duel start reuses the generic entry's existing handoff to Duel's Session. Admin powers, Undo and the seat switches apply to Haftgane games only. A rematch successor is a lobby with the old seat count.

**Alternatives.** (A) Two separate Home entries, with Duel untouched: the smallest change to Duel, but a group of two has to know that it wants Duel and find its entry. (B) Everything through the lobby, with Duel's one-tap starts removed: one way in for every game, but it costs one-tap bot games and changes more of Duel. The default keeps the one-tap starts and asks a group only for its size.

## Bots: information, work and lifecycle

Use the bot plan's honest observation/belief boundary, not its superseded aggregate room allowance. `Observe(seat)` includes the frozen public position and only that seat's own hand, previously held hands and legitimately seen private pile information. It excludes true opponent hands, other pending picks, real future deck/guild contents and unrevealed sold/tucked cards that seat never knew. Observation-derived samples, not cloned real hidden state, drive search. Test that two states differing only in forbidden information produce the same observation and decision.

Derive policy entropy independently from the setup root under a domain label, then derive the random tape from game id, policy version, logical point id, seat and the canonical permitted-observation hash. Never seed with the full state/rules hash, wire point reference, pending keys, task generation, wall clock or arrival order. Private observation hashes never go to frames, ordinary logs or downloads. Canonical observation encoding includes sorted remembered information so equivalent knowledge gives identical entropy. Domain separation is required even if setup and policy use the same root; bots receive the derived policy input, not the setup root or real hidden state.

For each bot at a point, persist effective policy version, difficulty/preset and fixed deterministic iteration allowance before launching work. Freeze those rows through restart. Accepted picks are restored without searching again or redistributing their allowance. Unaccepted bots keep their original allocation regardless of acceptance order. A bot switched into a seat without a pick receives the same fixed preset allowance, not a leftover share; persist that row first. Undo rebuilds the corresponding deterministic point inputs under the game's frozen configuration. Do not retain bot-result caches.

Apply Bot quality when calibrating allowances. Pool wait, pacing and pauses are outside deterministic work. A watchdog/cancellation is an exceptional safety path, not the normal way to exhaust a difficulty budget. Never accept a partially ranked incumbent based on elapsed time: use an explicit deterministic legal fallback, persist the actual canonical key and bounded diagnostic reason, and require zero watchdog fallbacks in normal acceptance workloads. Version presets when policy changes; supported persisted versions remain runnable or creation/load must fail explicitly rather than silently alter a pending decision's work.

At most one bot job is queued or active per room. Acquire the existing `ai.Pool.AcquireFor(ctx,key)`, search one pick, release with `ReleaseFor(key)`, then requeue for the next seat (`server/internal/ai/pool.go:103`, `server/internal/ai/pool.go:140`). Use the smallest attached human participant address, as Duel does, for fairness; spectators do not change it. Per-room serialization and existing address fairness remain. Duel's `ChooseFor` and separate review semaphore stay untouched (`server/internal/ai/pool.go:217`, `server/internal/room/review.go:427`). Do not introduce shared physical CPU/memory admission or a new deployment-wide one-search default.

Use one in-memory task generation to cancel/reject stale results, following Duel's bot generation pattern. Returned results carry logical point and seat; the actor also checks generation, current controller, pending vacancy and legal membership. Unrelated presence/revision changes do not invalidate valid work. Closing/undoing a point, a seat switch affecting a task or losing the last human participant cancels it promptly. Restart creates a fresh runtime generation; no old worker survives into it.

The client presents one grouped reveal and hand-pass sequence per simultaneous batch. Do not add six sequential human-visible bot dwells. Animation off still exposes the resulting state immediately. Future post-move explanations require a separate private, permitted-observation design; they are not part of this release or a shared Duel review rewrite.

## Protocol and lazy client entry

Duel requests, state, events and rejects have one contract on both versions. ## Review points not adopted

- **Major 7, reusing only a deterministic engine point after undo.** Keep the requested three-field submission and remove all extra counters/tokens/hashes, but retain a fresh wire reference derived from the existing revision. Duel's undo avoids this by incrementing version after replay (`server/internal/room/room.go:658`, `server/internal/room/room.go:680`), while seq retention is bounded (`server/internal/room/config.go:36`).
